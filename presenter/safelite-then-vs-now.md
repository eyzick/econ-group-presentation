# Safelite: 1993 vs. Today (Discussion Points)

Items marked **(verified)** were checked against a source listed at the bottom. Items marked **(general knowledge)** are reasoning, not sourced from the case, so present them as such.

---

## 1. Measurement is far better now (slides 4, 5, 6)
- **1993:** Safelite had just discovered it could count installs per tech. Effort was invisible, and output was the only signal.
- **Today (general knowledge):** GPS, app timestamps, job photos, and customer ratings make time, travel, and quality much more observable.
- **Discussion:** If you could observe effort almost perfectly, would you still need piece rate? Better measurement weakens the moral hazard problem but adds new ones: gaming the metric, surveillance resentment, and paying for what's easy to track instead of what matters.

## 2. Piece rate is now familiar work (slides 8, 14)
- **1993:** Gassiot said changing an industry built on hourly wages was "hard."
- **Today (general knowledge):** Gig platforms made per-task pay familiar, and algorithms now do the dispatching.
- **Discussion:** The techs' complaint that DCC managers assigned jobs "equitably" instead of to the fastest workers is what an algorithm would now do by default. It also raises the fairness questions on the compensation fairness slide.

## 3. Legal guardrails (slides 12, 14) (verified)
- **Federal:** Piece-rate pay is allowed, but the regular rate cannot fall below minimum wage. Overtime for piece-rate workers is computed from an average hourly rate (total weekly pay divided by hours worked).
- **California (AB 1513):** Piece-rate employers must pay separately for rest and recovery periods and other nonproductive time, shown separately on the wage statement, unless they pay at least minimum wage for every hour worked. The state fact sheet gives no exact effective date, so say "since about 2016" or leave the year out.
- **How to say it in the room:** "Piece-rate pay still has to clear minimum wage and overtime rules, and some states add more." The federal rules applied in 1993 too. The newer part is state-level add-ons.

## 4. Information and outside options (slide 13)
- **1993:** "Buck and a truck" poaching spread through word of mouth and recruiters.
- **Today (general knowledge):** Posted wages and job-board listings make the outside option visible instantly, so workers are likely more sensitive to small pay gaps.
- **Discussion:** This strengthens the retention risk of cutting the floor. It also changes the "30% pay cut" rumor problem, since a bad framing now travels fast.

## 5. The job is more complex (slide 16, multitasking and quality) (verified)
- **Today:** Safelite says "nearly all vehicle manufacturers require" the windshield camera to be recalibrated after replacement. Say "most manufacturers," not all.
- **Discussion:** Units per day was already a crude metric in 1993. Today a unit can mean very different amounts of work, so paying per unit is more prone to quality shortcuts.

## 6. Money has changed (slide 14) (verified, approximate)
- Prices are about **2.3 times** 1993 levels (third-party calculator using August 2026 CPI, not the BLS itself).
- $11/hr is about **$25.50/hr** today. The $440 weekly floor is about **$1,020**, the cut floor of $308 is about **$715**, and the $132 weekly drop is about **$305**.
- The household risk is the same, and the dollar amounts are larger.

## 7. What hasn't changed
- Output is still a noisy signal of effort.
- Wrong parts and bad scheduling still sit outside the tech's control.
- Insurance versus incentives is still the core tradeoff.
- If someone says the case is dated, the answer is that the technology changed, not the economics.

## 8. Optional reveal: what actually happened (verified)
The case stops before results. Lazear's later study of Safelite found a **44% increase in output per worker** (over about six months). Roughly half came from the incentive effect and half from changes in workforce composition. New hires after a year were about 24% more productive than comparable workers under the old system. Turnover rose slightly but was not selective, so most selection came through hiring. Profits also rose.

- **Use:** A good reveal after the final vote, since it supports the effort and selection slides. Showing it before the vote spoils the decision, so hold it for Q&A.
- **Careful:** The sources do not give a pay figure or a quality figure, so do not cite one.
